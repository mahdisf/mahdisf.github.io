### 6. Fuzzy AAN Knee Exoskeleton - Sit-to-Stand Transition Control

- **Date:** December 2023 in the accomplishment slides; the broader implementation evidence spans October 2023 to August 2025.
- **Sources:** `accomplishments_Cl.pptx` slides 8-9; `accomplishments.pptx` slide 8; the final manuscript `Fuzzy_AAN_NEXA_14040601.docx`; the older PDF `Fuzzy_AAN_NEXA_14040529.pdf`; ROS2 controller and CAN-node source files under `D:\my_work\FUM-CARE\NEXA\Codes\ROS2`; `STS_report.docx`; and the August 2025 cover letter. `accomplishment_create_bullets.txt` is only a generic writing prompt and adds no facts.
- **Related paper title:** "A Fuzzy Strength Index for Adaptive Assist-as-Needed Control of a Knee Exoskeleton Robot to Enhance Sit-to-Stand Transition."
- **Description:** Adaptive assist-as-needed control for sit-to-stand transitions, implemented on the FUM-NEXA knee exoskeleton. The final manuscript reports an engineering evaluation with four healthy subjects at slow, normal, and fast STS speeds; it does not report a clinical cohort.
- **Technology and methods:** A strength index derived from hip and knee velocity errors plus torque feedback; a nine-rule Mamdani fuzzy controller; desired sixth-degree velocity profiles fitted from 20 normal STS transfers; normalized knee torque reference; ROS2 on a Raspberry Pi 4B; Python/scikit-fuzzy/SciPy controller code; C++ SocketCAN motor and sensor nodes; and a lower-level PID torque loop. EMG is used for evaluation, not as a required controller input.
- **Role:** Project Manager and main author in the accomplishment record; first author in the final manuscript. The available code and documents support hands-on controller implementation, ROS2/CAN integration, experiment coordination, and technical writing, but do not support claiming sole ownership of the hardware or experiments.
- **Reported results:** With the 50% assistance setting, average IRMS reductions relative to the no-exoskeleton baseline were 30.34% at slow speed, 13.20% at normal speed, and 7.59% at fast speed; average SD reductions were 33.81%, 16.45%, and 14.10%, respectively. Wearing the exoskeleton without torque increased average IRMS by 41.21% (slow), 38.70% (normal), and 40.87% (fast). The manuscript's "low-strength" and "moderate-strength" labels describe controller output categories, not clinical diagnoses.
- **Relevance:** Highest combined evidence for robotic software engineering, robotic technical Product Management, and general technical Product Management. It includes a defined user problem, control design, hardware implementation, leadership, and quantified outcomes.
- **Evidence gaps:** The manuscript does not report inferential statistics, confidence intervals, end-to-end latency, actuator safety margins, comfort, long-term reliability, or a clinical evaluation. No public source-code repository, demo video, acceptance notice, or DOI was found in the supplied project files. Raw participant data should not be copied into the public blog.
